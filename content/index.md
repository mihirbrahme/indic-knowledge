# What were they actually asking?

India's textual traditions are quoted constantly and read rarely. This project
goes the other way round: it starts from what a text actually says, in the
language it says it in, and works outward from there.

Each entry opens with a question, shows the passage it rests on, explains it in
plain English, attributes the interpretations, and — only where it is honestly
possible — asks why it might still be interesting now.

## The method

Every entry moves through six stages.

**Discover** a question worth asking. **Original** — the passage itself, in
Sanskrit or the source language, with a literal translation. **Understand** what
it means on its own terms. **Perspectives** from the commentators who have
argued about it. **Connect**, where a modern parallel genuinely holds.
**Explore** further, because one entry should always open onto another.

The project keeps five things apart, and says which is which:

- what the original text says;
- traditional interpretation;
- historical and scholarly interpretation;
- modern conceptual parallels;
- modern scientific evidence.

An ancient idea is understood on its own terms before any modern comparison is
made. Similarity is not influence, and a good story is never allowed to outrun
its evidence.

## Where the texts come from

Behind the writing sits a frozen corpus of 116 approved works — from the Vedic
saṃhitās through the Upaniṣads, the Vedāṅgas, the six darśanas, grammar,
medicine, mathematics, astronomy, statecraft and poetics — and a registry of the
sources each one is read from.

Every passage records which witness was actually opened, which printed edition
it is anchored to, and where the chain is thinner than we would like. Where a
text has no reliable source, the project says so rather than quietly filling the
gap.

## The questions this project follows

Rather than academic categories, the material is organised around questions that
a reader can recognise:

- What is the self?
- How do we know?
- How does language mean?
- How does the world work?
- What makes life healthy?
- How should we act?
- How should society be organised?
- Why does art move us?
- How should we reason under uncertainty?

## What appears here next

The corpus, the source registry and the editorial system are in place. Entries
are being researched and verified now, and will appear here as they are ready —
each one with its passage, its sources and its caveats attached.

The aim is not inspirational quotation. It is to make the intellectual depth of
these traditions easier to find, and harder to misrepresent.
