## Original

ॐ केनेषितं पतति प्रेषितं मनः
केन प्राणः प्रथमः प्रैति युक्तः ।
केनेषितां वाचमिमां वदन्ति
चक्षुः श्रोत्रं क उ देवो युनक्ति ॥ १॥

## Transliteration

oṃ keneṣitaṃ patati preṣitaṃ manaḥ
kena prāṇaḥ prathamaḥ praiti yuktaḥ |
keneṣitāṃ vācam imāṃ vadanti
cakṣuḥ śrotraṃ ka u devo yunakti || 1 ||

## Working Translation

> Impelled by what does the mind fly out towards its object, sent forth?
> Yoked by what does the foremost breath go on its way?
> Impelled by what do people speak this speech?
> And the eye, the ear — which god yokes them?

This is the project's own literal rendering, kept deliberately close to the
Sanskrit. It is not a published translation, and it is marked `provisional`.

Two places where the Sanskrit admits more than one defensible English:

- **`kena`** is grammatically both "by what" and "by whom". The line does not
  settle which. Only the last quarter makes the reference personal, with
  `ka u devaḥ` — "which god". A translation that reads "by whom" throughout
  has quietly made a choice the first three quarters leave open.
- **`iṣitam`** is rendered "impelled". The root `√iṣ` also carries senses of
  seeking and desiring, so "willed" and "set in motion" are both available.
  "Impelled" is chosen because the verse's other verbs are verbs of sending
  and harnessing, not of wanting.

## Literal Notes

- **`kena`** — instrumental singular of the interrogative `ka`: "by what",
  "by whom". The word the text is named after.
- **`iṣitam`** — past participle of `√iṣ`, "impelled, set in motion, sent".
  The written `केनेषितं` is `kena` + `iṣitam`, joined by sandhi (`a` + `i` →
  `e`).
- **`patati`** — present third singular of `√pat`: "flies, falls, alights".
  A verb of motion. The mind is described as *going out* to its object.
- **`preṣitam`** — `pra` + `√iṣ`: "sent forth, despatched". Reinforces
  `iṣitam` rather than adding a new idea.
- **`manaḥ`** (`manas`) — the internal faculty that thinks, attends and
  intends. Conventionally "mind". In Sanskrit psychology it is **one faculty
  among several**, generally the one that coordinates the senses — not a
  single seat of the person, not the brain, and not "consciousness". See
  Claim Notes.
- **`prāṇaḥ prathamaḥ`** — `prāṇa` is breath, and more broadly the vital
  activity that breathing stands for; `prathama` is "first, foremost". Not
  oxygen, not respiration as a physiological process.
- **`praiti yuktaḥ`** — `pra` + `√i`, "goes forth", with `yukta`, past
  participle of `√yuj`: "yoked, harnessed, joined".
- **`vācam imāṃ vadanti`** — "they speak this speech". `vāc` is speech, and
  in Vedic usage also the faculty and the power of speech.
- **`cakṣuḥ śrotram`** — eye and ear, meaning the faculties of sight and
  hearing rather than the physical organs alone.
- **`ka u devaḥ yunakti`** — "which god yokes (them)?" `yunakti` is the
  present of `√yuj`, the same root as `yukta` two lines above.

The vocabulary matters. `iṣita`, `preṣita`, `yukta` and `yunakti` are all
words of **sending and harnessing** — the register of a driver and a team,
not of light or awareness. A rendering built on illumination ("what lights up
the mind?") has replaced the verse's own image with a different one.

## Claim Notes

- **TEXTUAL.** The verse asks, in the form of a question, what impels the
  mind, what yokes the foremost breath, what impels speech, and which god
  yokes eye and ear. It is a question. In this mantra the text gives **no
  answer** — the reply begins at 1.2.
- **TEXTUAL.** The mind appears here as one item in a list alongside breath,
  speech, eye and ear. The verse is not *about the mind* in particular; the
  mind is simply named first.
- **COMMENTARIAL.** None asserted. No registered source for this text
  provides commentary; see above.
- **EDITORIAL.** Any reading of the four quarters as a single regress
  argument about faculties is our framing, not a statement in the Sanskrit.
- **MODERN / EDITORIAL.** Any analogy drawn later between this question and
  a modern question about cognition is a comparison we are making. It is
  **not part of the Sanskrit text** and must never be presented as though it
  were.
- **The verse does not state a neuroscience theory.** It makes no claim about
  mechanism, organs or causation in anything like the modern sense, and
  nothing in it corresponds to a scientific finding.
- **"Consciousness" is a modern interpretive term, not a literal translation
  of anything in this mantra.** No word here means it. `manas` is a faculty
  of thought and attention; `prāṇa` is breath; `deva` is a god. Reading
  "consciousness" into any of them imports a category the verse does not use.
